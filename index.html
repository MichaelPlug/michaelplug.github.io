Publications

Public blockchains copy every record to every node. That's great for trust and bad for secrets. These two papers, written with researchers at Sapienza, Utrecht and TU Munich and published at CAiSE, encrypt documents so the ledger proves they exist while only the right people can read them.

MARTSIA

A Tool for Confidential Data Exchange via Public Blockchain

CAiSE 2025 · arXiv:2407.10684

Spreads trust across several independent authorities. Each one issues part of a decryption key and the reader puts the parts together, so no single party can unlock the data. One export document can be split so customs, the carrier and the manufacturer each read only their own part: one message instead of ten copies.

Runs on Ethereum and Algorand, and was tested with three existing apps for NFT trading, supply chain and retail.

  • Python
  • Solidity
  • PyTeal
  • IPFS
  • Ethereum
  • Algorand

CAKE

Sharing Slices of Confidential Data on Blockchain

CAiSE 2024 · arXiv:2405.04152

Encrypts a document, or separate parts of it, with a rule about who may read it, such as "customs on shipment 29837". The encrypted file goes to IPFS and its fingerprint goes on-chain. Only readers who match the rule get a working key.

Used in BRIE, a national cybersecurity project for import-export logistics.
